Since 2016

Three people, two vats, one arch

We started in a kitchen with a bucket of woad and got asked to leave. Everything since has been an argument about scale that we keep winning.

Why ten metres

A ten-metre bath is the largest amount of cloth two people can lift wet. That is not a philosophy, it is a fact about arms, but it turns out to be the right size for other reasons: you can see the whole piece at once, you can feel when the dye is exhausted, and if it goes wrong you have lost a day rather than a month.

Every scale above this one needs a machine to move the cloth, and a machine wants the bath to be the same every time. Making a natural dye bath the same every time means standardising the plant, which means buying extract instead of growing madder. We know where that road goes because we walked a mile down it in 2019.

How we got here

  1. 2 April 2016

    A bucket of woad

    Grown in a garden in Easton, fermented in a bucket, and it worked on the third attempt.

  2. 11 September 2017

    The arch

    Eleven pounds a square foot and a train every four minutes. The temperature never moves.

  3. 1 June 2019

    The extract year

    We bought indigo extract for six months because it was consistent. The cloth was consistent and dull, and we stopped.

  4. 15 March 2021

    Tomas and the loom

    Undyed cloth from eleven miles away instead of a container from elsewhere.

  5. 20 May 2024

    Water back into the vat

    A settling tank and a filter. Two thirds of the water in a bath is now the water from the last one.

  6. 1 February 2026

    The labels changed

    Dyestuff, mordant and number of dips on every label. It cost us one wholesale account and we would do it again.

Asked often

Can you match a colour exactly?

Close, and honestly close rather than claimed exact. Send us the thing and we will send you a swatch before we dye ten metres of it.

Is it colourfast?

Everything except the logwood, and the logwood says so on its own page and on its label. The table on the costs page has the tested numbers.

Do you dye customers' own cloth?

Yes, if it is a plant or protein fibre and it arrives clean. Polyester takes no natural dye at all, and a blend takes it unevenly, which some people want.

Where does the water go?

Two thirds back into the next bath, the rest to the drain. It is lime, sugar and plant matter; the pH is corrected first.

The extract year taught us the only thing worth knowing about consistency: you can have it, and it costs you the reason anybody wanted the cloth.

Rue Halloran, who signed the invoice
