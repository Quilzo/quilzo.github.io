Each agent is a principal, agent/NAME. What it writes is authored by that name, the audit log records it as the actor, and the access policy can grant it a role like anybody else. A sponsor is the person who answers for it: told when it misbehaves, and without whom it stops.

Give each agent a sponsor

  1. Name the person who answers for it

    quilzo agent sponsor returns-editor dana
    returns-editor is answered for by dana until 6 January 2027

    The sponsor must be somebody who can act in this installation. An identity lasts 90 days unless you say otherwise, and a year at most.

  2. See who answers for what

    The Answered for by column on Administration › Agents shows each sponsor and when the identity ends. An agent nobody answers for is marked nobody, with the command that fixes it.

  3. Renew it before it ends

    quilzo agent renew returns-editor --for 2160h

    Most agents nobody looks after are agents nobody needs. Renewal is when somebody decides which.

When an agent stops

What happenedWhat the agent does
Its sponsor's access ended, or the identity provider suspended or removed themDoes not run, and the Agents screen says the sponsor can no longer act here.
Its identity reached its end dateDoes not run until somebody renews it.
It was never given a sponsorRuns, and the security posture reports it (agent.no-sponsor) until somebody answers for it.
The shield paused itDoes not run until the pause ends or is lifted.

Evaluations still run in every case. They change nothing, and they are how a new sponsor finds out what they are taking on. Because the sponsor is checked against the same access the identity provider manages, deprovisioning a person through SCIM also stops the agents they answer for.

Prove what a run did

A receipt is every record of one run, taken from the hash-chained audit log, each with a proof that it is in the log, and a log head signed with Ed25519 and ML-DSA. Somebody holding only the receipt and your public keys can check it without access to your installation.

  1. Write the receipt

    quilzo agent receipt RUN -o receipt.json
    6 records of run run-20261008-f8cd3989, each with its proof, against a head of 52 signed by 0e667cb95d838091

  2. Publish your public keys

    The head's public keys are in head.pub.json, beside the audit log in the store. Put them somewhere the people who will check your receipts can fetch them independently, such as your own website.

  3. Check it anywhere

    quilzo agent verify-receipt receipt.json --keys head.pub.json
    verified  6 records of run run-20261008-f8cd3989 by returns-editor, steps [1 2 3 4]
  each is in the log under a head signed by 0e667cb95d838091

    Without --keys, the receipt is checked against the keys it carries, which proves only that it agrees with itself. Checking against keys you were given proves it came from that log.

A receipt includes what the run was asked, what it did, what it was refused, what it cost, whether it finished, and the memory it recalled and kept. Refusals are part of the outcome: a run that tried to publish four times and was stopped is a different product from one that did the work, and somebody paying per outcome is entitled to know which they got.

The same kind of receipt exists for an app's calls through one connection (quilzo apps receipt) and for what was forgotten about a person (quilzo memory receipt).

Grant an agent a role

Because an agent is a principal, the access policy can bound it whoever starts it:

quilzo auth grant agent/returns-editor author --on /help

A run is then limited by three things at once: the declaration, the agent's own role, and the person who started it. See People and access.
