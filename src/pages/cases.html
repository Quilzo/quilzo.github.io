Not part of Quilzo. A queue of cases and incidents, the reporting deadlines each law starts, and actions taken on other systems belong to the incident tooling your organisation already runs. Quilzo keeps no cases of its own.

The shield's request for a case

Some of the shield's playbooks end with a step that opens a case: a decoy was touched, a credential was in a prompt, a flaw reaches this build, the binary is not the one it says it is, a tool server redefined a tool somebody approved, or an agent keeps trying what it was refused, among others. That step keeps nothing here. It writes a record into the audit log, made by the service shield, with the action shield.open-case and what a case needs:

DetailWhat it holds
playbook, titleThe playbook that asked, and its title
signalWhat raised it, such as decoy or secret-in-prompt
subjectWhat it was about, such as an agent, a feature, a decoy or an advisory
severityhigh
summaryWhat happened and what the shield did, in a sentence or two; for a decoy, where it had been planted

The same playbooks usually tell the security contact too, by mail, at the mailto: address in security.contact. A playbook that is watching rather than acting records that it would have opened a case, and asks for none.

Open the case where you keep them

Stream the log to your SIEM with siem.stream.url (see Collecting events and Quilzo), and have it open a case from each shield.open-case record. In the OCSF it is sent, the action is activity_name, and the details are under unmapped as detail_title, detail_summary and the rest.

What the shield did in the meantime is on Protection › Shield, and quilzo shield list --all includes what has already ended. A protection that was a mistake ends with quilzo shield lift ID --mistake.
