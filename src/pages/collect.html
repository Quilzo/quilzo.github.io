Not part of Quilzo. Connectors that read your other systems, feeds they push, log collection, and searching and keeping those events belong to the SIEM or log pipeline your organisation already runs. Quilzo is one of its sources.

Stream the audit log to your SIEM

Set siem.stream.url and quilzo serve pushes the audit log every fifteen seconds, as batches of OCSF records that each follow the last exactly. A batch carries a tree head signed with Ed25519 and ML-DSA, and each request is signed as an HTTP message (RFC 9421), so the receiver can prove it holds every record, unchanged.

ToRun
Send to a SIEMquilzo config set siem.stream.url https://siem.example.com/quilzo
Give the receiver the keys to check what it is sentquilzo siem stream key --json
See how far it has got, and why it is failingquilzo siem stream status
Send what is waiting nowquilzo siem stream send

Nothing is lost while the receiver is down: the stream waits and carries on from the last record it took, and the posture check siem.stream-failing reports a stream that has failed for an hour. Clearing the setting stops it, without a restart.

Names, and a receiver elsewhere

People are pseudonymous in the log, and they stay pseudonymous in what is sent. siem.stream.reveal sends their names instead, and siem.stream.allow_remote lets the stream reach a receiver that is not on this machine or its network. Each is a weaker setting, so each takes two administrators: one asks, and another approves.

Or hand it over as a file

A SIEM that takes files can be given an export instead, as OCSF, CEF or JSON Lines, with an envelope of every event's hash beside it so the receiving side can tell whether anything was added, removed or reordered:

quilzo siem ocsf --envelope envelope.json > audit.ocsf.jsonl

What Quilzo's own agents reached around its model gateway is on the Fleet screen. What people across your organisation reach is in their proxy and DNS logs, which Quilzo does not read.
