Not part of Quilzo. Detection rules across your systems, correlating their events, hunting, and a queue of findings for analysts belong to the SIEM and security tooling your organisation already runs. Quilzo watches only for attacks on Quilzo, and sends that tooling its audit log as it is written.

What the shield watches for

Quilzo's servers raise a signal the moment they see something, and the shield counts them. Signals come from the servers themselves, so nothing a model reads can make the shield act. Among them:

SignalRaised when
signin-failuresSign-ins fail again and again from one source
admin-huntThe public site is asked for the admin's addresses
foreign-probeThe public site is asked for software it does not run, such as WordPress, .env or .git
chatbot-injectionPrompt-injection phrases are sent to a chatbot
decoyA decoy token is presented
agent-hijackedAn agent followed an instruction planted in what it read
agent-misbehavingAn agent or a connected app keeps trying what it was refused
secret-in-promptA credential was in a prompt on its way to a model, and was taken out
tool-changedA tool server changed a tool after a person approved it
self-exposure, self-flawA known flaw reaches this build (see Vulnerabilities and Quilzo)

Playbooks answer at once

A playbook says: when this signal comes from one source, or from anywhere, this many times within this long, do these steps, and do the next stage if it comes again. Its steps are the shield's own: block or slow a source, turn a public feature down, lock the admin to passkeys and single sign-on, pause an agent, cut a model route, tell the security contact, or ask for a case. Every protection ends by itself. A playbook acts, watches and records what it would have done, or is off.

Risky sign-ins

Each sign-in to the admin is judged against the person's own: travel nobody could have made, Tor or a hosting provider, a session copied to another device or country, an administrator in a country they have never signed in from. A risky one gets no session until its person confirms it with a passkey or a fresh single sign-on, and the security contact hears of the worst. This is signin.adaptive, on by default; turning it off is a weaker setting, which takes two administrators. See Sign-ins.

All of it in the audit log

Every response, every protection and every sign-in decision is written to the audit log. Stream it to your SIEM with siem.stream.url, and your own detections can use it alongside everything else they see (see Collecting events and Quilzo).
