Everybody manages their own passkeys under Administration › Passkeys. There is no password: no password storage, no reset flow and nothing to stuff.

Every passkey asks for the PIN, fingerprint or face that unlocks it, when it is added and at every sign-in, so one passkey is two factors. Touch ID, Windows Hello and a phone's screen lock already do this; a security key that has no PIN needs one set before it works here.

A token can carry less than the person holding it, never more. Session limits are organisation policy (see NIST parameters).

Two factors on every request

Every request a person makes to the admin carries two factors (NIST SP 800-63B-4, AAL2). A passkey is two on its own, and a sign-in through single sign-on counts as two. A token is one: anybody holding a copy has it.

  • Signing in by pasting a token gives a session that reaches nothing but Confirm it's you. Confirming with your passkey replaces it with a session that came through both.
  • A token sent in an Authorization header is a program's, and the admin's screens refuse it. Programs use the command line on the store's machine, the API or the agent interface.
  • A passkey is added from a session already signed in with two factors, so a copied token cannot add its holder's own passkey.

An installation is given fourteen days from the first time this version starts. Until then a token alone still reaches the admin, every page says when that ends, and the security posture names everybody with access who has no passkey and no single sign-on (mfa.no-second-factor). In those days a token's session may add its first passkey.

A link that adds somebody's passkey

Somebody without a passkey is given a link that adds one: quilzo auth enrol dana on the command line, or Give a passkey enrolment link beside them under Administration › People.

quilzo auth enrol dana
http://localhost:8080/enrol/qzenrol_…
  opened once, by dana, before 09:00 UTC on 22 October: it adds their passkey and does nothing else.
  • A link names one person, works once, and ends within a day (--for sets less). A newer link for the same person replaces the older one.
  • It adds a passkey for that person and reaches nothing else: no session is made, and they sign in with the passkey they have just added.
  • Only its hash is kept. Giving it, using it and ending it are recorded, with who gave it.
  • Guessing at links is throttled, and none works while the admin is locked down.
  • quilzo auth enrol dana --revoke ends a link nobody has used.

A passkey belongs to the address it was made on, and a browser refuses an IP address as one, so open the admin as http://localhost:8080 rather than 127.0.0.1, or set admin.base_url and links carry it.

Too many failed sign-ins

Failed passkey sign-ins to an account lock out the places they come from, not the person. The places you sign in from (the networks you have signed in from before) keep working, so nobody can lock you out by failing in your name.

  • Ten failures from places the account is not used from lock those places out of it for thirty minutes, and each lockout after that doubles, up to a day.
  • A hundred failures in a row, from anywhere, turn passkey sign-in to the account off until you sign in with single sign-on or another administrator unlocks it.
  • Each lockout is recorded and raises the account-locked sign-in signal. At your next sign-in you are told how many failures came before it.
  • Every other way in is slowed per address as well: the delay doubles after five failures in an hour, up to fifteen minutes, and nothing is accepted past a hundred. Throttling cannot be switched off. Its settings (auth.throttle.after, .ceiling, .base, .max and auth.lockout.alert) are held between bounds: at most ten free failures, a ceiling of 10 to 100, delays of a second to an hour, and an alert by twenty failures.
