1. Issue the SCIM token

    quilzo scim token

    Give the identity provider the SCIM address and this token. It lasts 180 days; --days sets up to 365. quilzo scim status, the Provisioning screen and the posture check scim.token-expiring say when it ends, starting a month ahead. Make the next one before then: an ended token is refused, and that stops the identity provider taking access away too.

    While a group is mapped to make administrators, or such a mapping waits for approval, a new provisioning token takes two administrators, because whoever holds it could make anybody an administrator by putting them in the group. Whoever asked receives it once: Receive the token on People, or quilzo pending redeem ID. Approving such a mapping lists every provisioning token that works now, who made it and how it was approved (quilzo pending show ID).

  2. Say what each group means

    quilzo scim map Editors author
quilzo scim map Security analyst
quilzo scim map "Lab technicians" lab-samples/technician
quilzo scim map Auditors auditor --until 2026-12-31

    A group means a role, a permission, a job, or a role an installed feature declares. A job granted for a time, such as auditor, is mapped with the day its access ends: --until on the command line (or --for 30d), or the screen's Until field: “For a job granted for a time, such as auditor: the day its access ends. Anything else lasts while somebody is in the group.” A mapping that now grants nothing, such as an auditor mapping with no day or one whose day has passed, is marked so, and what it gave is taken back when the admin starts. Mapping a group to administrator, or to a job that administers anything, waits for a second administrator, as any other way of making somebody an administrator does; it is listed under People › Waiting for a second administrator and by quilzo pending.

  3. Check it

    quilzo scim status

Only grants made by provisioning are ever changed by it; access granted by hand is left alone. Deactivation suspends a person at once: their sessions, tokens and connected apps stop, and agents they sponsor stop. Deletion also erases what agents remember about them. When a change that takes access away cannot be applied, Quilzo answers the identity provider with an error, keeps the person or group as they were, and the provider sends the request again; requests that arrive together are each applied in full, so a stale change cannot undo a deactivation that arrived beside it. A deactivation is heard however its attributes are spelled, and what cannot be applied is refused with an error rather than counted as done.

Provisioning never leaves the store without somebody who can grant access. The last person who can is not denied, even when the identity provider deactivates and renames them in one request: their sessions end, the suspension is recorded as failed, and the security contact is told.

Grants that provisioning makes are the identity provider's to review, since its next change would put back anything taken away here. What each group means here is reviewed in Quilzo, one item for each group (see Review who has access).
