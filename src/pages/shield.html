The shield is Quilzo protecting itself: playbooks that watch signals such as failed sign-ins, prompt injection against a chatbot or an agent following a planted instruction, and respond at once with protections that always end.

A playbook step that opens a case does not keep one here. It writes the request into the audit log, with the playbook, the signal, what it was about and a summary, for the security team's own tools to open the case from, such as a SIEM the log is streamed to (see the audit log, streamed as it is written). Incident cases and Quilzo says what the record holds.

It never acts against this machine itself, or a network added with quilzo shield trust add: an administrator's own address keeps the admin, a lockdown nobody could get past is not applied, and the command line on the machine always works. Any other machine, on a private network or not, is blocked like any other, and quilzo shield lift on this machine ends a block.

The admin's sign-in, its screens and the shield's own are never among them.

Who is told

Holding the playbooks (quilzo shield hold) stops the shield acting, not telling: a decoy touched during a hold is still told to the security contact, and holding and releasing are both told. Every alert is a mail to the mailto: address in security.contact, through the relay in mail.json. Without either, the posture finding alerts.unrouted says alerts reach nobody, and a mail that could not be sent is a failure on the record.
