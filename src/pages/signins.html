Protection › Sign-ins lists every sign-in to the admin in the last thirty days: who, when, where it appeared to come from, the device, how risky it looked and why. Above them are the sessions waiting for their person to prove it is them, and the people locked out.

A risky sign-in has to confirm it is its person

A sign-in is held when it shows any of these:

  • travel no person could have made since their last sign-in;
  • a session copied to another device or another country;
  • Tor, or a hosting provider rather than a home or office network;
  • an administrator in a country they have never signed in from;
  • three unfamiliar things at once, such as a new device, a new network and a new country.

The session sees Confirm it's you, and nothing else, until its person signs in again with their passkey or with a fresh single sign-on. The security contact is mailed about the worst of them. This is the setting signin.adaptive (NIST SP 800-53 IA-10). It is on by default, and turning it off is a weaker setting, which takes two administrators.

If somebody did not just sign in, This wasn't me on the same page ends every session they hold and tells the security team. A person without a passkey can ask an administrator, who gives them a link that adds one (Passkeys), or who lets the session in from Sign-ins once they are sure. Nobody can let in their own session.

Lockout

Failed sign-ins that belong to an account lock out the places they come from: after ten, for half an hour, and longer each time after that. The places the person usually signs in from keep working, so somebody guessing at an account cannot lock its owner out. A hundred failures in a row turn the person's passkey sign-in off until an administrator turns it back on. See lockout for what the person sees.

Where sign-ins come from

Name your offices and VPNs first, so a sign-in from them reads as yours:

quilzo geo network add 203.0.113.0/24 London --kind office
quilzo geo network add 198.51.100.0/24 "Staff VPN" --kind vpn
quilzo geo status

Other addresses are placed from a city database you download once: DB-IP's free IP to City Lite, in MMDB form, at .quilzo/geo/city.mmdb. Beside it, DB-IP's IP to ASN Lite names the network, so a hosting provider can be told from a home connection, and the Tor Project's exit list, refreshed daily, marks Tor. Quilzo never fetches any of them, and no lookup leaves the machine. quilzo geo help says where each comes from.

Every decision is in the audit log. When Quilzo streams its log to Vaigil, its rules raise a risky sign-in, one its person reported, and repeated failures and lockouts for the security team.
