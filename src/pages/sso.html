quilzo oidc configure --provider google --domain example.com
quilzo oidc configure --provider microsoft --tenant TENANT
quilzo oidc configure --issuer https://idp.example.com --client-id ID
quilzo oidc check

A sign-in finishes only in the browser that started it: starting one sets a cookie the provider's answer must come back with, so a sign-in somebody else began cannot be completed in your browser to put you in their account.

SAML

quilzo saml presets
quilzo saml add okta --metadata metadata.xml --url https://quilzo.example.com --preset okta
quilzo saml add okta ... --fingerprint SHA256   # trust its key, once you have checked it

Presets: Okta, Microsoft Entra, Google, JumpCloud, OneLogin, PingOne, AD FS and Keycloak. Single sign-on can be required, with named break-glass accounts that keep working when the provider does not. A requirement holds on every request, not only at the sign-in form: a session that did not come through the provider is refused, whichever way it was made.

When a sign-in counts as two factors

A single sign-on counts as two factors only when the identity provider says so, about a sign-in no older than session.max. Anything else is one factor: the session waits at Confirm it's you for the person's passkey. Somebody without a passkey here is given a link that adds one.

A SAML sign-in asks the provider for nothing: one that cannot meet a requested context refuses the sign-in. An OIDC sign-in asks for one no older than session.max (max_age), and for amr and auth_time by name where the provider reads the claims parameter. quilzo oidc check says whether it does, and quilzo oidc status what your provider needs.
